About Lovable 
Lovable is a Swedish AI startup founded in Stockholm in 2023 by Anton Osika and Fabian Hedin. The platform turns
plain-language descriptions into web applications and is designed to be accessible even to people with little

Lovable grew out of Osika&rsquo;s open-source project &ldquo;GPT Engineer&rdquo;, released in 2023. The project evolved into the
GPT Engineer App before being renamed Lovable in late 2024 and launching publicly that November.

The Promise 

Lovable can build impressively sophisticated web projects with AI, from static websites to full applications
with user management and database connections. But as soon as an application handles confidential information,
one question becomes crucial: Is that data protected against unauthorized access? 
 In its marketing , Lovable

projects . This can give the impression that the platform takes care of security

customers themselves. 
That leaves a troubling gap between the promise of an easy-to-use, all-in-one service and the responsibilities

Research Question 

We wanted to find out whether sensitive data in Lovable applications was accessible without logging in. We
looked separately at data sent when a website first loaded and additional content we checked by making further
read-only requests with the same API key. 
The result: We found many clear examples of sensitive data left unprotected. 

Methodology 

We focused on the database service Supabase . Its integration is relatively easy to
analyze, and we believe it is the most widely used database service among Lovable projects. Lovable&rsquo;s own
database features, introduced in late 2025, are still less widely used. Similar misconfigurations may occur
there too, but we did not examine them in this analysis. 

Using datasets from the open web archive Common Crawl , we identified and analyzed

containing email addresses in 10,560 tables across 7,403 of the 24,664 websites (30%).

Privacy, which companies were once happy to claim was all about personal choice, is now primed to affect everyone around you even more than it already did. 

Meta and Google’s smart glasses can record anything the owner is looking at or use artificial intelligence to describe it. Amazon’s Bee devices take that a step further and can record and transcribe everything. Dozens of other companies are introducing pendants , rings , and other products designed to record, transcribe, or summarize everything we say and do all day long. 

Privacy has always been a team sport. But moving those choices deeper into the physical world where strangers, colleagues, family, and friends are all now a part of your privacy choices is drastically changing the game. 

Tech companies want us to accept a reality where one person’s privacy choices (or lack thereof) means you suddenly need to be an expert in consumer tech who understands the capabilities of many devices on the market, how they work, how and if they signal when they’re recording, and what platforms they do or do not connect to. They believe these ambient surveillance devices that work in the background and are designed to look as normal as possible are the future, while they also expect the owners of these devices to use them responsibly and to notify those around them of their capabilities to act within whatever laws their states may have.  

This is not reasonable, nor how consent works in any practical sense.

Managing multiple sessions across a build used to require you to divide the work, juggle handoffs, and stitch the results back together. Now in a Claude Code project, you describe what needs to get done and Claude manages the work.
